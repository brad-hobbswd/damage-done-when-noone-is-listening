Four Week Church and Small Group Study

Study Curriculum

✧

A Scripture centered journey through the heart behind our words, the harm careless speech can cause, and the responsibility believers carry before God.

Prepared by Bradley Hobbs

Curriculum Overview

The Damage Done When No One Is Listening is designed for churches, Bible study groups, ministry teams, and personal discipleship. Across four weeks, participants examine private speech, the influence of the heart, the damage caused by gossip, and the responsibility to speak truth with love and integrity.

Duration

Four Weeks

One session each week, approximately 60 to 75 minutes.

Format

Teaching and Discussion

Scripture reading, guided teaching, honest reflection, and practical application.

Audience

Church and Small Groups

Suitable for adult Bible studies, ministry teams, and personal discipleship.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the study, participants should be able to:

  • Explain why private conversations remain accountable before God.
  • Identify how motives and heart conditions influence speech.
  • Distinguish destructive gossip from responsible reporting of genuine harm.
  • Recognize when spiritual language is being used to excuse harmful speech.
  • Practice a Scripture guided response that values truth, discretion, correction, and restoration.

Four Week Teaching Plan

Week One · Part One

God Hears What People Never Hear

Focus: Private speech, the heart, and accountability before God.

Core Scriptures: Hebrews 4:13; Matthew 12:34–37; Proverbs 4:23; James 1:19–20; Proverbs 10:9; Psalm 139:1–4.

Required reading: Part One and Chapters 1–3: The Quiet Rehearsal of the Heart, The Measure We Use on Others, and Accountability When No One Is Watching.

Goal: Help participants move beyond asking who heard their words to examining whether those words honor God.

Practice: Keep a private speech journal for the week. Record moments when frustration, assumptions, or the desire for agreement shaped a response.

Week Two · Part Two

The Tongue Can Destroy What the Hands Never Touch

Focus: The consequences of gossip, rumor, and careless speech.

Core Scriptures: James 3:5–10; Proverbs 18:8, 21; Ephesians 4:25, 29–32; Matthew 5:23–24; Galatians 6:1.

Chapters: The Damage Begins Before the Person Finds Out; A Person's Absence Is Not Permission; When Words Leave Wounds Behind.

Goal: Recognize how words shape reputations, relationships, and community trust, often before the person affected has a chance to respond.

Practice: Pause before repeating a story. Ask whether it is true, necessary, appropriate to share, and directed toward a constructive purpose.

Week Three · Part Three

When Gossip Dresses Itself in Spiritual Language

Focus: Prayer requests, spiritual authority, character, and integrity.

Core Scriptures: Matthew 6:5–6; Matthew 18:15–17; 1 Corinthians 13:1–7; Galatians 5:22–23; Matthew 7:15–20; James 3:13–18; Jeremiah 17:9–10; Psalm 139:23–24.

Required reading: Part Three and Chapters 7–9: Not Every Prayer Request Is an Act of Love, The Gift Does Not Excuse the Character, and When Spiritual Language Hides the Heart.

Goal: Evaluate whether spiritual language is protecting someone's dignity or disguising curiosity, accusation, resentment, or control.

Practice: Before sharing a concern as a prayer request, seek permission when appropriate and limit details to those who genuinely need to know.

Week Four · Conclusion and Personal Response

Let Your Words Reflect the Heart God Is Changing

Focus: Examine the heart, take responsibility for harmful speech, pursue appropriate repair, and commit to truthful, responsible, and loving communication.

Core Scriptures: Matthew 12:34–37; Ephesians 4:29–32; Psalm 139:23–24.

Required reading: Read the conclusion, Let Your Words Reflect the Heart God Is Changing, after reviewing the three parts and all nine chapters.

Goal: Bring the teaching together through honest self examination, repentance, responsible action, and a practical commitment to changed speech.

Practice: Complete the personal covenant in the conclusion and choose one specific action to carry out. Review progress after 30 days.

Recommended Session Rhythm

  1. Opening prayer and Scripture reading, 10 minutes.
  2. Teaching, 20 minutes. Introduce the main idea and explain the key passages in context.
  3. Group discussion, 20 minutes. Use the weekly questions and allow space for thoughtful answers.
  4. Personal application, 10 minutes. Identify one practical change to make during the coming week.
  5. Closing prayer, 5 minutes. Pray for humility, wisdom, repentance, and grace to speak truthfully.

Facilitators should protect confidentiality, avoid inviting participants to name or accuse other people, and direct specific allegations of abuse, threats, or misconduct to appropriate safeguarding or reporting channels rather than treating them as ordinary gossip.
