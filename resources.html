Resources & Further Study

The goal is not simply to learn what Scripture says about our words. It is to allow the Word of God to examine our hearts, change our conversations, and teach us to speak truth with wisdom, love, and accountability.

Begin With Scripture

Scripture Reference Library

Read each passage in context. Consider what it reveals about the heart, the tongue, truth, correction, and our responsibility toward one another.

Words and the Heart

Matthew 12:33–37, Proverbs 4:23, Luke 6:45

Examine the connection between what fills the heart and what comes out of the mouth.

The Power of the Tongue

James 3:1–12, Proverbs 18:21, Proverbs 12:18

Consider how speech can injure, mislead, encourage, or bring healing.

Speech That Builds Up

Ephesians 4:25–32, Colossians 4:5–6, Proverbs 15:1

Study the call to truthful, gracious, timely words that strengthen rather than corrupt.

Gossip and Slander

Proverbs 11:13, Proverbs 16:28, Proverbs 26:20–22, Romans 1:28–32

Notice the damage caused by spreading private matters, stirring conflict, and speaking destructively.

Correction and Restoration

Matthew 18:15–17, Galatians 6:1–2, Proverbs 27:5–6

Learn the difference between responsible, direct correction and conversations that needlessly expose another person.

Truth, Safety, and Accountability

Proverbs 31:8–9, Ephesians 5:11, Romans 13:1–4

Scripture does not require silence about abuse, threats, exploitation, or serious wrongdoing. Seek appropriate help and report concerns through responsible channels.

A Resource That Inspired This Study

Recommended Book

Resisting Gossip: Winning the War of the Wagging Tongue

Matthew C. Mitchell

Matt Mitchell’s book explores what gossip is, why it is tempting, and how Christians can respond through biblical wisdom and the transforming power of the gospel. It offers a useful companion for readers who want to examine their own speech and for groups addressing gossip within the church.

This book was an inspiration and a helpful point of reference for the broader conversation behind this teaching. The Damage Done When No One Is Listening presents Bradley Hobbs’s own teaching, emphasis, and application. Readers are encouraged to consult Mitchell’s work directly for his complete treatment of the subject.

Put Truth Into Practice

A Personal Speech Examination

Before repeating a story about someone, pause and ask whether it is true, whether you know it firsthand, whether sharing it is necessary, whether you are the right person to share it, and whether your purpose is to help or to harm.

When a concern involves abuse, danger, exploitation, or serious misconduct, do not use the warning against gossip as a reason to stay silent. Share relevant information with the appropriate person or authority, stick to what you know, and prioritize safety over reputation management.

When your words have caused harm, take responsibility without excuses. Correct false information where you spread it, seek to make things right where possible, and let changed behavior become part of your repentance.
