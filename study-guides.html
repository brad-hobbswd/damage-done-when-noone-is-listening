Personal Reflection and Group Discussion

Study Guides

✧

Use these four guides alongside the chapters, curriculum, and syllabus for The Damage Done When No One Is Listening. Each guide moves from Scripture to reflection and then into a practical personal response.

By Bradley Hobbs

How to Use These Guides

Read the assigned chapters and Scripture passages before the session. Answer the questions honestly, focusing first on your own choices and habits rather than using the discussion to identify other people's failures. A facilitator may invite group conversation, but no participant should be pressured to disclose private matters.

Allow approximately 45 to 60 minutes for each guide. Read the passages in context and use the questions to support reflection, not to replace the meaning of Scripture.

Study Guide One · Week One

God Hears What People Never Hear

Read: Part One and Chapters One, Two, and Three.

Scripture: Hebrews 4:13; Matthew 12:34–37; Psalm 139:1–4; Proverbs 10:9.

Observe the Word

  1. What do these passages reveal about God's knowledge of our actions, words, and motives?
  2. According to Matthew 12:34–37, what connection does Jesus make between the heart and the mouth?
  3. How can repeated conversations shape our attitudes toward another person?
  4. What does Proverbs 10:9 teach us about integrity, including the choices we make when no one is watching?
  5. Why must we examine our own conduct before judging someone else's?

Examine Your Heart

  1. Do you ever speak more freely about someone when you believe they will never hear what you said?
  2. When you repeat a concern, are you seeking a solution, wise counsel, or agreement with your frustration?
  3. Have you ever judged another person's motives without separating what you observed from what you assumed?
  4. Does your private conduct agree with the values you profess publicly?
  5. What motive, attitude, or habit of speech might the Lord be inviting you to examine?

Personal Application

For the next week, pause after significant conversations and reflect on what motivated your words. Consider whether your speech was truthful, necessary, and fair, and whether repeating the conversation helped resolve the concern or strengthened resentment. Examine your own assumptions and consider whether your private choices reflect the integrity you profess publicly.

Write down one pattern you noticed and one practical change you want to make. Keep your reflection private unless you freely choose to share it. Remember that examining your heart does not mean ignoring genuine harm or remaining silent about abuse or misconduct. Seek appropriate counsel and take responsible action when necessary.

Prayer focus: Ask the Lord Jesus to reveal the motives of your heart, correct your judgment, strengthen your integrity, and help you bring your private words and actions into agreement with His truth.

Study Guide Two · Week Two

The Tongue Can Destroy What the Hands Never Touch

Read: Part Two and Chapters Four, Five, and Six: The Damage Begins Before the Person Finds Out, A Person's Absence Is Not Permission, and When Words Leave Wounds Behind.

Scripture: James 3:5–10; Proverbs 18:8, 21; Ephesians 4:25, 29–32; Matthew 5:23–24; Galatians 6:1.

Observe the Word

  1. Why does James compare the tongue to a small fire with the potential to cause great damage?
  2. What kind of speech does Ephesians 4:29 call believers to practice?
  3. How can repeated or unverified words damage a person's reputation before that person knows what has been said?
  4. What do Ephesians 4:31–32 and Matthew 5:23–24 teach about responding when our words have caused harm?

Examine Your Heart

  1. Have you ever repeated a story without knowing whether it was accurate or necessary? What responsibility did you have before passing it on?
  2. How can you respect an absent person's dignity while still seeking appropriate counsel or reporting a genuine concern?
  3. When someone tells you your words caused harm, are you more likely to listen or to defend your intentions?
  4. What might responsible repair require beyond saying, “I'm sorry”?

Personal Application

Before repeating a story, ask: Is it true? Is it necessary? Is this the right person to tell? Am I sharing it for a constructive purpose? If you discover that you have spread something false or caused harm, consider what correction is needed and who needs to receive it. Offer a specific apology where appropriate, respect the other person's boundaries, and demonstrate change through your conduct.

Responsible repair does not mean pressuring someone to forgive you, demanding that trust return immediately, or concealing abuse or misconduct. Serious concerns should be taken to appropriate people or safeguarding channels.

Prayer focus: Ask the Lord Jesus to govern your speech, expose careless assumptions, give you humility to accept correction, and guide you in repairing harm truthfully and responsibly.

Study Guide Three · Week Three

When Gossip Dresses Itself in Spiritual Language

Read: Part Three and its three chapters.

Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:1–3; Galatians 5:22–23; Ephesians 4:15.

Observe the Word

  1. Why does Paul place love at the center of spiritual expression and service?
  2. Which qualities in Galatians 5:22–23 should shape how believers speak about others?
  3. How can truth be spoken with love without hiding or excusing wrongdoing?

Examine Your Heart

  1. Have you ever shared more personal information than was needed under the label of a prayer request?
  2. How can curiosity or frustration hide behind words such as concern, discernment, or accountability?
  3. Who genuinely needs to know when a serious concern must be addressed?

Personal Application

When a prayer request involves another person, protect their privacy and share only what is appropriate and necessary. When harm or misconduct requires action, speak to the proper person or safeguarding channel instead of spreading the information through informal conversation.

Prayer focus: Ask God to bring your words, motives, gifts, and character into agreement with His truth and love.

Study Guide Four · Week Four

Let the Heart Be Changed Before the Words Are Released

Read: Review all three parts and your previous reflections.

Scripture: James 1:19; Ephesians 4:31–32; Colossians 4:6; Matthew 12:36–37.

Review and Reflect

  1. What have you learned about the connection between your heart and your words?
  2. Which speech habit do you most need to change?
  3. If your words have harmed someone, what would responsible repentance and repair look like?
  4. How can you pursue accountability without humiliating others or avoiding necessary truth?

Write Your Personal Speech Covenant

Complete these statements in your own words:

  1. I will stop or reduce the habit of...
  2. Before I speak about someone who is absent, I will...
  3. When I need to address wrongdoing, I will...
  4. If I cause harm with my words, I will take responsibility by...
  5. For the next 30 days, I will practice...

Personal Application

Choose three specific commitments and review them weekly for the next 30 days. The purpose is not to claim perfection, but to practice honesty, humility, correction, and consistent growth.

Prayer focus: Ask Jesus Christ to transform the heart, guide the tongue, and help you pursue truth and peace without compromising righteousness.

Facilitator Reminder

These guides are intended to encourage personal examination and responsible Christian speech. They must not be used to silence people who report abuse, threats, exploitation, or misconduct. Appropriate concerns should be shared with people or agencies equipped to respond. Confidentiality should be respected, but it should never be used to conceal harm or prevent necessary protection.
