The Damage Done When No One Is Listening

What Our Words Reveal When We Think No One Can Hear

Foundational Scriptures
James 3:5–10 · Matthew 12:34–37 · Ephesians 4:29–32

Introduction: The Conversations We Think Do Not Count

There is a side of our spiritual lives that we rarely discuss because it does not always happen in the sanctuary, does not always happen behind a pulpit, and does not always happen in the presence of people who would challenge what we say. It happens in the quiet moments after someone has walked away, in the private conversations between trusted friends, in the messages we send when our emotions are running high, and in the discussions where another person's name becomes the subject of our attention. These are the moments when we may believe our words are harmless because the person we are discussing is not present to hear them, defend themselves, or explain what actually happened.

Yet the absence of an audience does not mean the absence of accountability, and the absence of the person being discussed does not mean the absence of consequences.

We must confront a question that reaches far beyond our ability to control our mouths: Who are we becoming in the conversations we believe nobody will ever hear?

It is easy to examine our conduct when we know someone is watching. We may choose our words carefully when the pastor is standing nearby, when a respected leader is sitting at the table, or when the person we have been tempted to criticize is within hearing distance. We understand that certain words might expose our intentions, so we restrain ourselves until the opportunity to speak freely presents itself. However, the real condition of our hearts is not revealed only by how we behave when others can see us. It is also revealed by what we permit ourselves to say when we believe there will be no consequences for saying it.

This is where the conversation about gossip becomes a conversation about holiness, because holiness cannot be reduced to the appearance we maintain in public while our private conduct contradicts the character of the God we profess to serve.

James 3:8

But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.

James 3:9–10

Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God. Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.

The warning is not limited to profanity, angry outbursts, or openly hostile speech. It reaches into the conversations in which we use our words to diminish another person's dignity while maintaining the appearance of spiritual respectability.

We can speak the name of Jesus in worship and speak carelessly about His people after worship. We can lift our hands in surrender while refusing to surrender our opinions, our resentments, and our desire to have the final word about somebody else's life. We can speak passionately about holiness while ignoring the holiness required in the way we handle another person's reputation.

The issue is not whether we know how to sound spiritual. The issue is whether our speech is being brought under the authority of the One we claim to worship.
