Four Week Syllabus

✧

The Damage Done When No One Is Listening: What Our Words Reveal When We Think No One Can Hear.

Author and study resource: Bradley Hobbs

Course Description

This four week biblical study examines gossip, private speech, the motives of the heart, and the responsibility of believers to use words in ways that reflect the character of Jesus Christ. Participants will study Scripture, discuss realistic situations, and develop personal practices for truthful, gracious, and accountable communication.

Course Objectives

Participants who complete the course will be able to:

  1. Describe the biblical principle of accountability for words spoken in private and in public.
  2. Examine the relationship between the heart, motive, and speech.
  3. Identify common forms and consequences of gossip, rumor, and careless reporting.
  4. Explain why a prayer request or spiritual concern does not automatically make sharing personal information appropriate.
  5. Apply biblical principles to conflict, correction, confidentiality, and restoration.
  6. Create a personal plan for more responsible speech.

Participation and Assessment

  • Participation: Engage respectfully in Scripture reading and discussion.
  • Weekly reflection: Complete the personal practice assigned each week.
  • Final application: Submit or privately complete a personal speech covenant with three measurable commitments.
  • Growth standard: Assessment emphasizes thoughtful engagement and personal application, not public disclosure of private experiences.

Discussion Questions by Week

Week One

  1. Why do people sometimes speak differently when they believe no one else will hear?
  2. What is the difference between examining a harmful action and attacking a person's character?
  3. How can we invite God to expose our motives without using self examination to excuse another person's wrongdoing?

Week Two

  1. How can a repeated story change as it passes from person to person?
  2. What harm can occur before the person being discussed even knows about the conversation?
  3. When must a concern be reported to someone with responsibility to act?

Week Three

  1. What makes a prayer request appropriate, respectful, and necessary?
  2. Why must character and accountability accompany spiritual gifts or public ministry?
  3. How can we tell the difference between a sincere concern and spiritual language used to disguise resentment?

Week Four

  1. What personal speech pattern most needs attention?
  2. What does responsible repair look like after harmful words have been spoken?
  3. What boundaries or habits will help maintain change over the next 30 days?

Facilitator Guidance

Lead with Scripture, humility, and care. Do not turn the study into a forum for naming people, revisiting rumors, or pressuring participants to disclose sensitive experiences. Distinguish gossip from appropriate reporting of abuse, threats, exploitation, or other serious misconduct. Concerns that require action should be directed to the appropriate safeguarding, pastoral, organizational, or legal channel.

The purpose is not to silence every difficult conversation. The purpose is to help believers speak truth responsibly, refuse needless harm, and pursue correction and restoration with integrity.
