Why spiritual gifts and public ministry must be accompanied by godly character and accountability.

By Bradley Hobbs

The Gift Does Not Excuse the Character

As Oneness Apostolic believers, we understand the importance of repentance, baptism in the name of Jesus Christ, the infilling of the Holy Ghost, and a life that reflects the holiness of God. These are not subjects to be treated casually, because they concern our response to the revelation of Jesus Christ and our commitment to live according to His Word.

Yet we must never allow our emphasis on spiritual experience to become a reason for neglecting spiritual character. Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of the Spirit, including love, peace, patience, gentleness, goodness, faithfulness, meekness, and self control. These qualities are not separate from our spiritual lives; they are part of the evidence that the Spirit is transforming the way we live.

If we can speak about the power of the Holy Ghost while refusing to exercise self control in our conversations, we need to examine whether we are allowing the Spirit to govern our conduct as thoroughly as we claim to desire His power. If we preach repentance to others but refuse to acknowledge our own destructive speech, we have created a contradiction between the message we proclaim and the life we practice.

The issue is not whether believers will ever make mistakes with their words. Every one of us needs the grace of God, and every one of us must remain teachable. The issue is whether we are willing to be corrected when the Word exposes our failures or whether we use our spiritual identity to avoid accountability.

The name of Jesus must never become a covering beneath which we excuse conduct that contradicts His teaching. Our confession should lead us toward greater humility, greater honesty, and greater responsibility for the way we treat other people.

If Jesus is Lord of our lives, His authority must reach the conversations we have when nobody else is listening.

Personal Application

Take a few quiet moments to consider how this teaching applies to your own words, choices, and relationships. What is one specific response you can practice this week?

Scripture for reflection: Galatians 5:22–23; Matthew 7:16–20.
